Modern script vs older scripts

The benchmark is split into two halves that count the same. The older half averages four script stages with equal weight, so Tamil-Brahmi counts as much as pre-reform print.

Reading the two halves

Each row compares one system's modern-script and older-script reading scores. The gap is modern − older on paired bootstrap replicates; ≈ marks a gap whose 95 % interval includes zero.

Where each script is hard

Script × medium

A script's score is the mean over the reading tracks it appears in of the mean of its subsets there. These grids show every slice: the stone and copper-plate subsets are split by the script on each item.

Script & medium identification

Identification by era

Script identification is scored per class: the modern half is the F1 of “modern Tamil”, the older half the mean F1 of pre-reform Tamil, Grantha–Tamil, Grantha and Tamil-Brahmi. Calling a Malayalam page “pre-reform Tamil” costs the older half. Medium identification is split by the script on each item.

Diagnostic · not ranked

How systems fail, by era

The failure modes that a character error rate hides, split into the modern and older halves, and three controls: invented words, perturbed famous texts and surfaces with nothing to read.

Failure modes

Share of reading items in each half. Wrong-script means letters from a script the references never use; line tests check that every reference line appears, in order (olmOCR-style), on multi-line items.

Reading vs guessing

Score on real text and on phonotactic nonce words in each half. A reader that relies on its language model drops on nonce words.

Prior pull on perturbed famous texts

Tirukkural couplets, Sanskrit verses and Tamil-Brahmi lines with a few letters changed. −1: the answer follows the image; +1: it reverts to the text as usually written; near 0 with a low reading score: nothing was read. Values above 0 are marked.

Invented text on blank and effaced surfaces

Paper, boards, palm leaves, stone, copper and potsherds with nothing legible. The right answer is “[no text]”; the table shows the share of items answered with letters.

Can the numbers be trusted?

Reliability

Signal-to-noise per script and subset, human reference readers, and how much a score moves when the prompt is reworded or the run repeated.

Signal-to-noise

Signal is the spread of a score across evaluated systems; noise is its bootstrap standard error. Below about 1 a score cannot yet tell those systems apart.

Human reference readers

Expert readers on the same items, shown for scale and never ranked.

Prompt wording and repeat runs

Spread of a system's scores over three paraphrases of every prompt, and over repeated runs of the same prompt.

For analysis

Get the data

Everything on this page comes from one file. The per-script CSV lists every system's score per era, script stage, script × track cell and script × subset slice, with intervals and item counts.

Files

  • data/leaderboard.json — every score, interval and diagnostic
  • data/scores-by-script.csv — long format, one row per score
  • results/<system>/v1-test/scores.json and predictions.jsonl in the repository — raw answers

Fields in models[].scripts

score, ci95
Reading score of the script stage, 0–100, with its cluster-bootstrap 95 % interval

Mean of the script's subset slices within each reading track
subsets
Each slice: score, interval and item count (stone and copper plates hold two scripts)
identification
Script-ID F1 and recall for this class; medium-ID accuracy on its items

Modern script, or the mean of the older script stages
era_gap
